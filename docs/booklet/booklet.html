Part I

Getting started

What the platform is, who does what, what it lets us do, and how to sign in and keep work flowing when there is no signal.

1The platform at a glance

Two tools share one set of records. Crews use the field app on their phones in the yard, on site and at the truck. The office uses the office site in a web browser. What one does, the other sees as soon as the phone has synced.

Who does what

RoleWorks inCan
Field worker CrewField appScan, receive deliveries, count, move and issue stock, pack and move crates, start a load for a truck that turns up, check drivers in, load out, depart once the licence is on file, unload and finish stops at our own yards and sites.
Office staff OfficeOffice site, and the appEverything a crew can, plus: places, site maps and crate labels; planning loads; letting a truck leave without the licence (with a reason); confirming deliveries at outside shops and vendors; marking pieces found, writing them off, closing and cancelling loads; driver licences; exceptions and holds; importing the expected list; shipping stock out.
Administrator AdminOffice siteEverything office staff can, plus users and access, projects, the Data Browser, the admin audit, correcting material records, editing or deleting PO & Installation links, and undoing a write-off.

The screens only hide buttons a person can't use; the database itself enforces every role (appendix A5). When this guide says office, it means office staff and administrators alike.

Projects

Every record belongs to a project, and each person works only in the projects they are assigned to. Two things are shared by every project: places (yards, sites, outside shops and vendors) and crate labels, whose HU- codes are unique across all projects.

  • Active: people can work in it.
  • Completed: read only. Pages say “This project is read-only.”
  • Archived: hidden from everyone; its records are kept.
Practise in Training YardTraining Yard is a practice project with its own place, site plan, racks and crate labels. Practise there, and never in Main Yard, North Site or South Site: practice scans in a real project become real records.

2What the records let us do

The platform is only as good as the scans behind it. When crews scan as they work and the office keeps the setup current, these are the questions it answers.

Fewer lost items

Every tag and every crate has one current answer to “where is it?”: in an area of a yard or site, inside a crate, on a truck, missing from a truck, or gone (issued, shipped or written off). When a stop is finished, anything due there that wasn't scanned off is marked missing on that load, so the search starts the same day, at the right truck and stop. A write-off needs a reason.

Better reconstruction planning

Counts and receipts record what each item is, its category (for example Gas Turbine 2 › Lube Oil › Cooler), its condition with photos, and the area of the site plan it sits in. Before a work package starts, the office can see what is on hand for it and where, instead of sending crews to look.

Clearer priorities for loads and refurbishment

Outside Shops lists what is at each shop, since when, and which load took it there. The Loads board shows what is planned, loading, in transit and delivered. Items that have sat at a shop longest, and pieces still waiting for a truck, are in plain view when deciding what moves next.

Planned receiving laydowns

Site plans divide each yard and site into named areas, nested as deep as needed (Laydown A › Bay 3), with racks linked to areas. Deliveries, counts and sealed crates all land in a named area, so the office can see what each laydown holds and plan where the next deliveries go.

Fewer logistics delays

A truck can't leave until the driver is checked in with the licence on file, or the office records why it may leave without. Stops run in order, the printed manifest travels with the driver, and the crew at each of our yards and sites can see what is coming before the truck arrives, then scan it off on arrival.

Accountable records

Every change records who made it and when, and corrections, write-offs and holds record why. Crews see their own activity; the office sees everyone's.

The dealEach of these depends on two habits: crews scan at the moment things happen (on the truck, off the truck, into a crate, into an area), and the office keeps places, site plans and racks up to date. Part IV sets out both.

3Signing in and choosing a project

The field app

  1. Open the app. It shows “Loading session...”, then a card titled Sign in with the line “Laydown yard management”.
  2. Enter Email and Password and tap Sign In. Forgot password? sends a reset link to your email.
  3. Crews land on Scan. Office staff and administrators land on Dashboard.

The first sign-in on a phone needs a connection. After that, someone who signed in online on that phone can reopen the app offline on “previously verified access”; uploads wait until access can be checked again.

Choosing a project. The app opens the project last used on that phone, or else the first one assigned. The project name is at the top right; with several projects it is a button that opens Select project. Drafts and saved work are kept separately for each account and project. With no project at all the app shows “Project access pending”; ask an administrator, then tap Check access again.

Tabs. Crews have Scan, Count, Loads, Inventory and Activity. Office users have Dashboard, Materials, Exceptions, Locations, Scan, QR Codes, Activity, Reports and, for administrators, Data Browser; they reach Count and Loads from the Dashboard cards Initial field count and Loads.

Signing out. Tap the sign-out icon at the top right and confirm Sign Out. Read chapter 4 first: signing out deletes any driver licence that hasn't uploaded yet.

The office site

New people are invited by an administrator (chapter 19).

  1. Open the email “Finish your Invenio invitation”. Its code lasts one hour and works once, and only the newest email's code is valid.
  2. Press Finish account setup. On “Enter your email code”, fill Invited email address and Email code, then press Verify code.
  3. Choose a password of at least 8 characters, enter it twice and press Update Password.

After that, open the office site from the link in your invitation email and bookmark it; it opens on “Sign in” (Material Status Report). Forgot password? sends a reset code by email; enter it, then set a new password. A wrong or old code shows “This code is incorrect, expired or already used.” Someone with no project sees “Access pending” and a Check access button.

The header has the active-project selector (it asks before leaving unsaved work), Search records..., a theme switch and a bell that opens the Work Inbox. Your email and Sign Out are at the foot of the sidebar.

4Working offline and Sync

The app saves everything on the phone first and uploads it, in order, whenever it has signal.

When there is no signal, a yellow bar reads “You are offline — changes will sync when reconnected”, or “You are offline — 3 pending”. Every tab has a Sync & recovery link at the top, with a count when anything is waiting. When the database refuses a step, the banner says so, for example “1 queued action refused: open Sync to decide”.

What works without signal

  • Works offline: scanning crate labels, counting (with the data saved the last time Count was opened online), packing crates, every step of a load, and moving and issuing stock.
  • Needs signal: a fresh sign-in; the rack list in Receiving, unless a rack was carried over from the count; the material page's “Where”; Activity.

The Sync & recovery screen

The top shows the project, “N pending submissions” and Sync now. Each item shows what it is, when it was saved and one of these states:

StateMeaning and what to do
Saved on deviceWaiting for signal. Nothing to do.
Upload interruptedThe connection dropped mid-upload. It retries by itself, or tap Upload now.
Needs attentionFive tries failed. Tap Retry upload when the signal is good, or discard it; ask the office if it keeps failing.
Refused: needs a decisionThe database said no and gave a reason. It is never retried by itself. Fix it (Fix in Count, Move it to this load), or discard it. A refused driver licence can only be discarded and recorded again in Driver check-in.

A discard can't be undone. Support details shows the submission ID, tries and last error to pass to the office.

Loads upload in order

A load's steps upload strictly in the order they were made. One refused or stuck step holds back the later steps of that load, and Depart and Finish stop stay blocked, with a View Sync link, until it is retried or discarded. Steps that are only waiting for signal don't block, so a truck can be loaded and sent off with no signal at all. Tap Done on a load-out or unload screen before switching project, or the last few scans can be lost.

The driver's licence on the phone

The licence number, issuing state and expiry are kept only in the app's memory until they upload: if the app closes first, they must be typed again. The photos are temporary files, deleted after upload, after a discard, and at sign-out. Sync never shows the licence number or the driver's phone number.

Before you sign outSigning out deletes every driver licence and licence photo that hasn't uploaded. Other saved work stays on the phone for that account and project and uploads at the next sign-in. Check Sync first.

5Scan

CrewOffice

Scan is where most work starts. Point the camera at a tag or a crate label and the app opens the right screen for it.

  1. Aim at the label, or tap Enter Code Manually, type the Code Value and tap Submit. After each scan the camera pauses; tap Scan Again. Without camera permission the app asks for it (Grant Permission).
  2. The app decides what the code is and opens the matching screen (table below).
  3. Pack a crate (or Continue packing HU-…) starts packing; see chapter 9.
You scanThe app opens
A crate label (HU-…)The crate screen, from the phone's copy, even offline (chapter 9). Capitals don't matter.
A received itemIts material page (chapter 8).
A counted or expected item, or a tag new to this projectReceiving (chapter 6). A counted tag arrives filled in, with a banner such as “Counted Oct 30 in Zone B”, and the rack linked to that area already chosen.
A tag that can't be received nowA message under the camera, for example “… is on load L-0003; receive it when it comes off.”, “… has been received and is no longer in stock.” or “… was written off on a load; an administrator can undo that.”

Tags and projects. An item tag is unique within its project, whatever the capitals, so a tag from another project counts as new here. Crate labels are unique across all projects: another project's label shows “This label hasn't been packed yet”, and packing it is refused with “This label belongs to another project”.

OfflineWith no signal, or no answer within five seconds, any item tag opens Receiving and the database checks it on upload. A tag that was already received comes back refused in Sync with “This tag has already been received”. Discard it there.

6Receiving a delivery

CrewOffice

Receiving records a delivery at the gate: what came, against which order, its condition and photos, the decision, and the rack it goes to. Start by scanning the item's tag.

The header shows “Step n of 7” with the step's title and “QR:” with the tag.

  1. Material details. Choose the Material Type (Pipe, Fitting, Valve, Flange, Structural Steel, Plate, Tubing, Bolt/Fastener, Gasket, Electrical, Instrumentation, Other) and fill Quantity, Size, Grade, Weight (lbs), Description and Spec.
  2. PO / delivery. Vendor, PO Number, Delivery Ticket #, Carrier, all optional.
  3. Photos. Choose the Photo Type (General, Damage, Delivery Ticket), then Take Photo or From Library. Photos are optional but always worth it for damage.
  4. Inspection. Condition Good, Damaged or Mixed (Damaged and Mixed ask for Damage Notes), and Inspection Pass?
  5. Decision. Accept, Partial Accept (enter the accepted quantity, more than 0 and less than delivered) or Reject. Damage or a failed inspection flags an exception automatically; otherwise answer Flag Exception? and optionally choose the type (Wrong Type, Wrong Count, Damage).
  6. Location. Pick the rack, shown as “Zone - Row r, Rack k”; hold racks carry a HOLD AREA badge. Tap Review receiving.
  7. Review. Check each section (each has Edit) and tap Submit receiving. The app shows “Receiving saved”, then “Receiving synced” once the receipt and photos are uploaded.
Finish one receipt at a timeAn unfinished receipt can't be picked up again later: scanning another tag starts a new one. The rack list also needs signal, unless the rack came over from the count.

Holds are not set here. A flagged exception goes to the office, which decides to hold, return or release the stock (chapter 18).

7Counting what is already here

CrewOffice

The initial count records what is already in a yard or on a site, tag by tag: what it is, its category, where it is on the site plan, and a photo. The office can import the list of tags it expects first (chapter 17), so the count shows what is still to find.

Count home

The top line shows when the data was last updated, for example “Updated Oct 30 14:05 · saved for offline use”. Tiles show Expected, Counted, Received and “N of M tags found”, which adds “· 1 needs attention in Sync” when a count was refused or stopped retrying. A refused count isn't counted as found, and its tag goes back to Not found yet. Not found yet lists expected tags still to find, with a search box; tapping one counts it without scanning. Recent counts shows each count's state.

Counting a tag

  1. Scan the item's tag. The QR value is the tag number. The banner says whether it is an expected item, a new tag (added to the list) or already counted (saving updates that count).
  2. Item. Fill Item name, Quantity and Unit (EA, FT, LF, LB, SET, BOX, PAL), plus Part number, Condition, Description and Notes as needed. Tap Next: category.
  3. Category. Search or browse and tap Use “…”. If the right category is missing, tap Propose new category here; the office reviews it, and the item is saved under it now.
  4. Location. Choose the site plan if there are several. Tap the plan where the item is: the pin drops and the smallest area under your finger is chosen. Or pick from the area list. An area is required when the plan has areas; the pin is optional.
  5. Photo and review. Take a photo (optional, but it helps the office confirm the item), check the summary and tap Save count. Count next keeps the category and location for the next tag.

Tags the count refuses

  • “HU- codes are crate labels; count the items inside the crate.”
  • “Tag … has already been received, so it doesn't need counting.”
  • “… is packed in HU-…; take it out of the crate before counting it.”
  • “… is on load L-0003; count it when it comes off.”
Before going out of signalOpen Count once while online. It saves the categories, site plans and expected tags on the phone; without that it says “open Count once while online”.

8Stock: moving, issuing, correcting

CrewOffice

Received material lives on the Inventory tab (office: Materials). Each item opens on its material page, which says what it is, how much is left and where it is. Search finds an item by its type, its grade or part of its QR code.

The page shows Quantity, Size, Grade, Weight, Spec, QR Code, Rack, Where and Received. “Where” reads, for example, “On load L-0003”, “Missing from load L-0003”, “In crate HU-ABC123 (inside HU-XYZ789) at Yard · Rack 2” or “Place · Area”; crate and load lines open those screens.

Actions

ActionWhat it asks
TransferA New Location (hold racks marked HOLD) and an optional reason. Then Confirm Transfer.
IssueJob Number, Work Order (optional) and Quantity to Issue. Then Confirm Issue. Issuing all of a packed item takes it out of its crate; issuing part leaves the rest packed and opens the crate.
Ship Out OfficeDestination, Carrier, Tracking Number and Quantity to Ship. Then Confirm Shipment.
Correct where it isRecords where an item really is, without counting it again: choose the Place, then an area, an optional reason, and Save the location. For items in the yard that aren't packed.

Each action goes to Sync, and the app says “Saved”. A greyed-out action says why in a yellow box, for example “… is packed in HU-…; take it out of the crate first.” or “… is on load L-0003; it can be moved, issued or shipped when it comes off.”

Holds and returns

When the office puts stock on hold or returns it to the vendor (chapter 18), crews see “… is on hold; the office releases it before it can be issued.” Until it is released it can't be packed, loaded or issued, and it can only be transferred to a hold area; the location list then shows hold racks only.

9Crates

CrewOffice

A crate is anything that holds pieces and moves as one: a crate, skid, bundle, basket or container. Each has its own HU- label, printed by the office. A crate is never split: it goes to one stop, and comes apart only when it is unpacked.

Packing a crate

  1. On Scan, tap Pack a crate and scan the crate's label. A new label becomes a crate the first time it is packed.
  2. Details. Choose the Type, add a description, and switch on High value if it is; everyone who scans it will see that. Tap Next: scan items.
  3. Items. The camera keeps scanning; the counter shows how many are in the crate. Each scan answers, for example “… added.” or “… is already in crate HU-…; take it out of that crate first.” An item must be counted or received before it can be packed. Scan another crate's label to put that crate inside.
  4. Location. Choose the Place, then the area, from the list or by tapping the plan. A crate inside another crate goes wherever the outer one is.
  5. Review. Tap Seal crate when it is closed up, or Save open to keep adding later. Sealing needs something inside, and an area wherever the place has a site plan with areas. Changing a sealed crate opens it again.

The crate screen

Scanning a label opens the crate: its status (Open, Sealed, On a load, Delivered, Unpacked, Missing, Written off), High value if set, where it is (“Inside HU-…”, “On load L-0003”, “Delivered to …”, “Last at …”) and its contents. The buttons are:

  • Pack / add items: add more, or tap Take out on a row, then save.
  • Move crate…: choose a new place and area and tap Save the move. Nothing is unpacked and a sealed crate stays sealed.
  • Unpack…: the items come out where the crate stands, and the label can be packed again.
Crates inside cratesCrates nest up to five deep. An inner crate travels with its outer crate and can't be moved or loaded by itself. A crate that is planned on a load, on a truck or missing can't be packed, unpacked or moved until it is taken off the load, unloaded or found.

10Loads on the phone

CrewOffice

A load is one trip by an outside carrier's truck: where it starts, the stops it makes, the driver and companies, and every crate and loose piece on it. The office usually plans loads on the website; crews check the driver in, scan pieces on, send the truck off and scan pieces off at our yards and sites.

The Loads tab

The tab lists open loads (planning, loading, in transit). Each card shows the route, progress such as “4 of 10 on board” or “3 delivered, 5 on board, 1 missing”, the driver and the date. New load starts one for a truck that turns up unplanned: fill Starts at, the date, the stops (each Drop-off, Pick-up or Both) and the truck details, then Save load. It works offline; the load gets its number (L-0001…) when it uploads.

The load screen shows Pieces, the Route (each stop Done, Arrived or To come) and Truck and driver, with the licence state: “Licence on file”, “Licence saved on this device”, “Licence incomplete”, “No licence recorded yet” or “Left without a licence (the office gave a reason)”.

Driver check-in

  1. Driver. Driver’s name and Driver’s phone.
  2. Companies. Up to three, “from the one we hired to the one whose truck it is”: name, role (Broker, Carrier, Dispatcher, Other), contact and phone.
  3. Licence. Licence number, Issuing state, Expiry, a photo of the Front of the licence and, if useful, the back.
  4. Review and Save check-in. From then on the licence shows only as “Licence on file”; nobody in the field can read it back.

Loading out

  1. Tap Load out and scan each piece as it goes on the truck. The counter reads “12 of 14 on board” and the list ticks each piece.
  2. A piece that isn't planned asks where it comes off: tap a stop, or Leave it off. A piece planned on another load that hasn't left can be moved to this one.
  3. Tap a ticked row to take a piece off again. Scan the crate, not the items packed inside it.
  4. Tap Depart… when the truck is ready.

Departing

The screen asks “Ready to leave?” and lists any planned pieces that will stay behind. It won't let the truck go until the driver's name is in, the licence (number and a photo of the front) is on file or waiting to upload, something is on board, and no earlier step of this load is stuck in Sync.

No licence, no departureCrews can't send a truck without the licence. Only the office can let it leave without, and has to give a reason (5 to 500 characters), which is recorded on the load with their name.

Unloading at a stop

  1. Tap Unload at a stop and choose the stop, for example “1. Gulf Machine · Drop-off · 3 due”.
  2. Scan each piece as it comes off. At our own yards and sites, choose the Area where the pieces go, from that place's site plans. The app answers “… off.”, or flags it: wrong stop, an extra piece that wasn't on the list, or a missing piece now found.
  3. Tap Finish stop…. The app lists any pieces due here that haven't been scanned off: they will be marked missing. Tap Finish stop, or Keep scanning.

At outside shops and vendors, crews scan off what they hand over but don't finish the stop: “… is an outside shop or vendor: scan off what you hand over, and the office confirms the stop.” A piece scanned off after its stop was finished is still recorded; a missing one is then found.

What only the office does: let a truck leave without the licence, finish stops at shops and vendors, change stops after the truck leaves, ship stock out; and, on the website only, plan pieces, confirm pieces without scanning, mark pieces found, write them off, and close or cancel loads.

13Places, site maps, areas and racks

Office

Where things are rests on three layers: places (the yard, site, shop or vendor), site maps (a plan of one yard or site, divided into named areas), and racks, the older receiving locations, each linked to an area.

Places

Places are shared by every project. Everyone sees the list and the map; office staff edit. Nothing is deleted, and names are unique among active places.

  1. On Places, press Add place. Choose the Kind (Yard, Site, Outside shop, Vendor, Other) and a Name. A yard or site can belong to one project, or be shared by every project.
  2. Fill the Address, Contacts and Notes.
  3. Under Map point, click the map, drag the pin or type coordinates. Set the Arrival radius (metres) (25 to 20,000; 300 if left blank) for trackers later.
  4. Press Add place, or Save changes when editing.

Retire takes a place out of use for new crates and loads; it stays on record and Restore brings it back. If someone else edits the same place while your form is open, the page tells you to reopen it rather than overwrite their change.

Site maps and areas

  1. On Site Maps, press New map. Give it a name, choose the Place it draws, and upload the Plan image (PNG, JPG or WEBP). Press Create map.
  2. Press Draw area and click each corner, then Finish area. Name it, pick a colour, choose what it sits Inside (another area, or the top level) and its Rack, and Save area.
  3. Use Add area inside… on a row to nest areas, for example Laydown A › Bay 3.

Areas are never deleted. Make inactive hides an area, and everything inside it, from crews. A plan tied to a place supplies the areas used at that place; a plan tied to no place can be used at any of the project's yards and sites.

Racks

Each area links to at most one rack, and each rack to one area. Receiving still records a rack; the link lets the count, crates and receipts all point at the same area. Racks without an area lists any rack not yet linked: work through it until it is empty. Racks themselves are added in Data Browser › Locations (appendix A7); include at least one hold-area rack.

14Crate labels and the crates list

Office

Crate labels are printed in batches before crews need them. A label becomes a crate the first time a crew packs it.

  1. On Crates, press Print crate labels and enter How many labels? (1 to 500; six fit on a Letter page).
  2. Press Reserve and print. A print window opens; each label is a large QR code holding only its code (for example HU-7K3QX9), printed underneath.
  3. The page confirms the range reserved. Print the last N labels again reprints the same batch without new codes, until the page is reloaded.

If pop-ups are blocked, nothing is reserved: allow pop-ups for the site and try again.

The list shows every crate the crews have packed, with its status, where it is (“Inside HU-…”, place and area, or “On load L-…”), contents and when it was sealed. Filter by status or search by code or description; the drawer lists the items and any crates inside.

15The Loads board

Office

The Loads page is where the office plans trucks and follows them: columns for Planning, Loading, In transit and Delivered, then Closed and cancelled.

Each card shows the load number and date, “From … · Next: …”, the driver, piece counts, and chips such as “N flags”, “Started in the field”, “Left without licence” and “Licence on file”. Loads that crews started appear in Started in the field so the office can check them.

Planning a load

  1. Press New load, fill the details and press Create load. The drawer opens with its sections: Status, Details, Companies, Stops, Manifest and Driver's licence.
  2. Details: origin, planned date, truck type and numbers, bill of lading, driver name and phone, notes. Save details.
  3. Companies: up to three, from the one we hired to the one whose truck it is. Save companies.
  4. Stops: Add stop for each place, with its kind (Drop-off, Pick-up, Drop-off and pick-up) and date; reorder with the arrows; Save stops. Up to 20 stops.
  5. Manifest: enter a crate code or item tag, choose where it Comes off at (and, for a pick-up, where it is Picked up at), and press Add to the plan. Tick the box to move a piece from another load that hasn't left.

Each section saves on its own and keeps unsaved edits in the others. On a load a crew started, that crew plans the stops until the truck leaves.

Departure

  • Depart with what's on board: once pieces have been scanned on. Planned pieces that weren't scanned on stay behind.
  • Mark loaded and depart: only when the truck starts at an outside shop or vendor, where nobody scans; it marks every planned piece loaded.
  • Without the licence, either button opens Let it leave without the licence: write why (5 to 500 characters) and press Leave without the licence. The reason, your name and the time show on the load.

On the road

Each stop shows “Due · Delivered · Missing · To pick up” and its state (Pending, Arrived, Done). Change stops changes, moves or removes stops the truck hasn't reached; removing the last one left delivers the load.

Outside shops and vendors. Nobody scans there, so the office confirms: Mark delivered, Confirm pick-ups or Mark delivered and picked up, depending on the stop. This also finishes the stop. At our own yards and sites the crew finishes the stop from the phone.

Missing pieces

A piece due at a finished stop that wasn't scanned off is missing. If a crew scans it off later, at any stop, it is found. Otherwise:

  • Mark found: say where it was found, with an optional note.
  • Write off: say why. Writing off a crate writes off everything inside it.
  • Undo write-off Admin: say why it is put back. The piece is missing again, and a closed load reopens.

Closing and cancelling

Close load is offered once every stop is finished and nothing is missing. Cancel load is offered only before the truck leaves; crates already on it go back to how they were. Neither can be undone, except that undoing a write-off reopens a closed load.

Print manifest prints the load, driver and phone, truck, bill of lading and companies, one table per stop (tag, piece, high value, checked) and signature lines for shipper, driver and receiver. It never includes licence details.

16Driver licences

Office

We record the driver's licence for every load so that, if something goes missing, we know who drove the truck. It is the most sensitive data on the platform: only office staff can see it, it is never printed, and it is removed automatically when it is no longer needed.

Recording and checking it

Crews record the licence at check-in (chapter 10). In the load drawer, office staff see the Driver's licence section and can also enter it themselves:

  • Number: Licence number, Issuing state and Expiry date, then Save licence. The page repeats only the last four characters (“…ending 1234”), and only for numbers longer than six characters.
  • Photos: Add a photo (JPEG or PNG, up to 10 MB), up to four per load; Delete photo removes one.
  • Remove it early: Remove the licence asks why, for example a wrong licence.

Never print, export, photograph or copy a licence outside the platform, or send its number by email or message. Crews can add a licence but can't read one back, by design.

How long it is kept

A daily job removes each licence, its number and its photos, 365 days after the load closes (for a load that never closes, 365 days after the last activity on it). Thirty days before that, the Loads page shows office staff a panel, Driver licences due for removal in the next 30 days, covering all their projects.

  1. Press Show the N licences to see each load and its date.
  2. To keep one, press Keep, write why (for example “the driver still has our GPS tracker”) and press Keep the licence. It won't be removed until office staff release it.
  3. When the reason is resolved, press Release. If the date has passed, the next daily run removes it.

The same Keep and Release buttons, and the removal date, are in the load drawer. A kept licence can't be removed early until it is released. When office staff open Loads, the page also removes any licence that is due and not kept, as a backup to the daily job.

17Importing the expected list

Office

Before an initial count, the office can import the tags it expects to find, so crews can see what is still missing and every expected item arrives with its details.

  1. On Initial Inventory, press Import expected tags and choose a spreadsheet (.xlsx) or CSV file, up to 20 MB and 5,000 rows.
  2. Match the file's columns to the fields. Tag is required; Not imported skips a column.
  3. Press Preview import. This is a dry run: any problem blocks the whole file until it is fixed.
  4. Press Apply import. The page reports how many tags are new, refreshed and skipped, and how many categories were added.

Tags already counted or received are never overwritten. A category path such as “Gas Turbine 2 › Lube Oil › Cooler” creates any missing levels. Tags are unique within a project whatever their capitals.

The page shows tiles for Expected, Counted, Received and New, filters by category, area (an area includes the areas inside it), status and text, a table or map view, and Export CSV.

Categories

Inventory Categories holds the tree. Add, rename, reorder and archive categories; Merge… moves one category's items and subcategories into another and archives it. Categories that crews proposed while counting are listed under Proposed by field crews, each with Approve, Rename and Merge into….

18Exceptions, holds and outside shops

Office

The Work Inbox

Deliveries flagged at receiving (damage, a failed inspection, wrong type or count) land in the Work Inbox. Show open, mine, overdue, unassigned or all; each row has an owner, a due date and a status.

  1. Press Review to open the next decision.
  2. Choose an Owner (office staff on the project) and a Due date.
  3. Choose the Decision and add notes, then Save decision:
    • Hold — keep open: the stock is on hold.
    • Returned to vendor — close: closes the inbox item; the stock stays marked as being returned.
    • Released — close: the only decision that frees the stock.

Stock on hold or being returned can't be packed, loaded or issued, and crews can move it only to a hold area. Release it as soon as it is cleared.

Outside Shops

The Outside Shops page lists, for every place of kind Outside shop, the crates and loose items that loads delivered there and that are still there: the code or tag, the piece, There since and Brought by (a link to the load). Filter by shop or search. Use it to chase work at shops and to plan the next pick-ups. Shops never scan; their status changes when a load is confirmed delivered or picked up.

19Users, projects and access

Admin

Users & Access

  1. Invite: press Invite User, fill Email, Full name, Role (Field worker, Office staff, Administrator) and Project access, and Save User. The person gets the invitation email (chapter 3).
  2. Edit: Edit changes the role and projects. Untick Active account to deactivate someone; they lose access at once.
  3. Pending invitations: Resend sends a new code (only the newest works); Cancel invite also removes the person's project access.

Filters cover role, status (active, inactive, invitation pending, expired, cancelled) and project. History opens that person's entries in Activity & Audit. The last active administrator can't be removed.

Projects

New project opens the form: name, description and status (Active, Completed — read only, Archived — unavailable), then Save project. A new project is assigned to the administrator who creates it; reload a working page to see it in the project selector. A project can't be completed or archived while any of its loads is open.

Who can open what

Every page needs an active account, an accepted invitation and at least one project that isn't archived. Without a project, people see “Access pending”. Administrators also have the Data Browser and the admin audit (appendix A7).

21Rules for crews

  1. Scan as it happens. On the truck, off the truck, into a crate, into an area: scan at that moment, not later from memory.
  2. One label, one thing. Never put a crate label on an item or reuse a tag. Tell the office before re-tagging a damaged tag.
  3. Record where it really is. Choose the area the item is actually in. Found it elsewhere? Use Correct where it is; don't count it again.
  4. Seal it where it stands. Seal a crate when it is closed up, where it stands, and move it in the app when you move it in the yard.
  5. A crate goes whole. Take items out only through the app. A crate goes to one stop; if its pieces go to different places, unpack first.
  6. Nothing on the truck without a scan. Scan every piece on. If one comes back off before the truck leaves, take it off in the app too.
  7. No licence, no departure. Check the driver in with the licence number and a photo of the front. If the driver won't, call the office; never wave the truck off.
  8. Finish a stop only when it is done. Scan everything off first. Anything not scanned becomes missing, and someone has to go and find it.
  9. At shops and vendors, the office confirms. Scan off what you hand over and tell the office; don't try to finish the stop.
  10. Hold means hold. Stock on hold or going back to the vendor stays in a hold area. Never pack, load or issue it.
  11. Clear Sync every day. Before the end of each shift, open Sync: retry what failed, fix or discard what was refused, and ask the office when unsure. Never sign out with a licence still waiting.
  12. Licences stay in the app. Never photograph a licence with the phone's own camera, write its number down or send it on.
  13. Your own account only. Sign in as yourself, never share a login, and sign out of a shared phone.
  14. Practise in Training Yard. Never practise in a real project: practice scans become real records.

22Rules for the office

  1. Set up before the work starts. Places with map points, a site plan for each yard and site, areas named the way crews talk about them, every rack linked to an area, and crate labels printed.
  2. Plan the load before the truck arrives. Stops in order, every crate and loose piece planned to the stop where it comes off, and companies recorded.
  3. Leaving without the licence is the exception. Use it only when there is no other way, write a reason that will make sense in a year, and get the licence afterwards if you can.
  4. Confirm shop and vendor stops the same day. Your confirmation is the only record of what was handed over there.
  5. Resolve missing pieces quickly. Scan, mark found (with where) or write off (with why) within the week, while people still remember.
  6. Close loads when they are done. A delivered load with nothing missing should be closed; closing starts the licence's 365-day clock.
  7. Keep a licence only for a reason. For example to recover a tracker the driver kept. Release it when the reason is resolved.
  8. Every exception gets an owner and a date. Decide holds and returns promptly, and release stock as soon as it is cleared.
  9. Keep access tidy. Invite people with the right role and projects, deactivate leavers the day they leave, and review project access when a project ends.
  10. Change production only through a release. Never edit the live database by hand (appendix A8).
  11. Never print or export licence data. The manifest leaves it out on purpose.

A4How a change reaches the database

Every change from the field app is a step: a call to apply_field_operation(operation_id, project_id, action, payload). The office site uses the same function for logistics actions, so both follow the same rules.

  • Exactly once. Each step carries an ID made on the phone. The database locks that ID; if it has applied it before, it returns the saved result, so a retry after a dropped connection never applies twice. The same ID with a different request is refused.
  • All or nothing. The action's checks, its changes and its audit entry run in one transaction. A refused step changes nothing.
  • Actions. receiving, count, transfer, issue, shipment, correct_material, exception; and for crates and loads pack, unpack, move, relocate, load_save, load_plan, load_on, load_depart, load_off, stop_complete, load_close, line_found, line_write_off, line_write_off_undo.
  • Batches. Load-out and unload send scans in batches. The pieces the database turns down come back as separate refused steps while the rest are saved.
  • Times. Each scan carries the time it happened on the phone, so steps that upload late still record when the work was done. Comparisons with server times allow five minutes for phone clocks.

A5Security and privacy

Access

  • Row-level security is on for every table. People read only the projects they are assigned to; places are readable by every active user.
  • Writes go through checked functions that test the role and project themselves (is_office_user, has_project_access, can_work_project). The screens hiding a button is a convenience, never the control.
  • History is never deleted: places retire, areas go inactive, loads close; the audit log can't be changed. The one exception is an administrator deleting a PO & Installation link, which takes its status history with it after the audit log has recorded both.
  • Completed projects are read-only and archived projects are hidden, in the database as well as on screen.

Driver licences

  • Licence tables and the driver-licences bucket are readable by office staff only. Crews can upload a licence but can never read one back, in the app or by any other route.
  • Office staff see photos through signed links that expire after five minutes. Licences are never printed; the manifest leaves them out.
  • The audit log keeps at most the last four characters, and only for numbers longer than six.
  • On the phone the number stays in memory only, and the photos are deleted after upload, after a discard and at sign-out.
  • Each licence is removed 365 days after its load closes unless office staff keep it with a reason. The daily job removes it with the service-role key; opening the Loads page does the same as a backup.

Accounts

  • Invitations and resets use email codes that last an hour and work once; only the newest is valid.
  • Deactivating someone takes effect at once. The last active administrator can't be removed.
  • The admin function accepts requests only from the production office site.
  • Secrets are never kept in the repositories. The service-role key exists only in the repository's secret store and the function's secret store; set it there yourself and never paste it into a chat, file or ticket.

A8Releasing changes

Each part of the platform has its own release path. Database releases and app updates reach every user at once, so each one needs the owner's go-ahead.

What changedHow it is released
DatabaseA new migration file in the MSR repository, applied to production with the guarded procedure below.
Office siteMerge the pull request to main; the web host deploys it and the smoke test checks it.
Admin functionDeployed from the office repository with the database host's command-line tool, token checks on.
App screensAn over-the-air update to the production channel.
App native partsA new store build, only when native modules or the app version change.

Database migrations

  1. Write the change as a new numbered migration file in the office repository. Never edit a migration that has run in production, and never change production by hand.
  2. CI builds a fresh database from every migration and runs the database tests (tests/database/run.sh).
  3. Rehearse on a copy at the previous version, then run the tests again.
  4. Fingerprint production with the schema fingerprint check (read-only) and compare it with a fresh build at the previous version. They must match line for line; --detail finds any difference.
  5. Back up, then apply in one transaction: check that production's latest migration is the one before this; record the migration with its full text; check the stored text's checksum; run it. Any error rolls all of it back.
  6. Fingerprint again, compare with a fresh build at the new version, and record the release.
  7. Release what depends on it: the office site, the admin function, the app update.

Office site

The web host runs the site build, which copies the pages and scripts, checks that the required pages exist and writes release.json with the commit and SHA-256 hashes of the key assets. The production-smoke job then waits for that commit to go live, checks the asset hashes and confirms private paths return 404. To roll back, restore the last verified deploy on the web host.

Field app

App updates carry new screens to every phone on the same runtime (1.1.0). A phone downloads an update when the app opens and switches to it the next time the app is fully closed and reopened. The current production update is 56cc444c on build 5.

Licence job

A scheduled job in the office repository runs the licence removal script every day at 09:17 UTC, and can be run by hand. It needs the service-role key as a repository secret, which an administrator sets; the database address is optional. If a run fails, the Loads page's backup still removes due licences when office staff open it; fix the job the same week.

Planned. Trackers will be attached to crates and high-value items, not trucks. A server job will record positions and use each place's arrival radius to mark crates as arrived at shops and stops, with a map on each load and a warning when a tracker in transit goes quiet. Before building it, confirm with the tracker provider that the tags will be seen at outside shops, since they report only near a gateway.

Purchase orders and shipments

The purchase-order import script checks an MSR workbook and, only with --apply, a --project-id and the service-role key, imports both sheets in one transaction. Without --apply it only validates. It never deletes rows.

A10Known limits in this edition

Things that work differently from what you might expect, with the way round them. Each is a candidate for a later update.

Field app

  • Receiving needs signal for the rack list, unless the rack came from the count, and an unfinished receipt can't be resumed: scanning another tag starts again.
  • Count has no high-value switch and no place picker.
  • Delivered loads aren't on the Loads tab; open them from an item's or crate's “Delivered to … on L-…” line.
  • Moving a crate, correcting a location, departing, finishing a stop and editing a load close the screen without a confirmation message; Sync shows the step.
  • A planned date or licence expiry can't be cleared once set.
  • The app can put a receipt on hold but not release it; release it on the office site.

Office site

  • Activity & Audit shows administrator changes only; operational history is in Data Browser › Audit Log.
  • Racks can be added only in Data Browser › Locations.
  • Print the last N labels again is lost when the page reloads; reprint from the reserved range before leaving the page.
  • The arrival radius has no effect until trackers are connected.

Updating this guide

The guide is built from docs/booklet/ in the MSR repository: booklet.html (the text), booklet.css (the print style) and diagrams/. When the software changes, edit the text, add a row above, update the edition lines on the cover, and run the booklet build (the command is in docs/booklet/README.md).

It prints the guide to docs/booklet/Invenio-Field-Platform-booklet.pdf and numbers the contents. Keep the contents to one page.
